TL;DR

A robot reads an IKEA manual with an off-the-shelf vision-language model, plans where to inspect its work and when to ask a person, and then assembles the product in physics simulation, inspecting, repairing and asking for help only where the plan says it pays off.

How it works

Read the manual

An off-the-shelf VLM answers one yes/no question per part and manual step.

Decode a plan with risk

A decoder combines the answers into an assembly plan with an error probability for every step.

Synthesize the behavior tree

A dynamic program places inspections and operator queries at minimum cost under a bound on undetected failures.

Execute with skills

Every assembly action expands into a skill-level tree whose skills run only inside success regions measured in simulation.

Results in the paper

42%assembly operations recovered by the decoder on 21 products (8B VLM), vs 21% for a single plan query
5×fewer undetected failures from asking the operator about likely misread steps
14–21%lower cost than inspection-only rules at equal risk
9 / 10runs complete the SUNDVIK chair frame in simulation
